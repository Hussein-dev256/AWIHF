Official Medical Outreach Impact Documentation

One Day, 301 People: What Your Support Made Possible at Abwoch

Date: 3 July 2026 • Location: Abwoch Health Centre III, Tochi County, Omoro District • Theme: “Rooted in Community, Rising in Health: The AWIHF Story”
The official pre-launch medical outreach of the Acholi Women in Health Foundation proved that an integrated, partner-led community healthcare delivery model effectively addresses critical rural healthcare barriers.

1. Executive Summary & Context

On 3 July 2026, the Acholi Women in Health Foundation (AWIHF) held its official pre-launch medical outreach at Abwoch Health Centre III in Tochi County, Omoro District. The outreach was conducted as part of AWIHF's pre-launch programming, designed to demonstrate the Foundation's service model and reach underserved communities before our formal public launch.

The camp brought five essential health services to the community, free of charge, through close collaboration between AWIHF, health-service partners, Abwoch Health Centre III, and local district authorities. By the end of a single day, 301 community members were reached, validating the immense demand for localized, dignified healthcare in Northern Uganda.

4. Detailed Clinical Findings

A. General Medical Consultations

General consultations were the busiest service point, with 121 individuals seen (40.2% of the camp attendance). Women represented 71.1% of these consultations. Seventy-one (71) consultations concluded with a documented clinical diagnosis and immediate treatment dispensing.

  • Respiratory illness: 13 diagnoses (18.3%)
  • Hypertension: 13 diagnoses (18.3%)
  • Urinary tract infections (UTI): 13 diagnoses (18.3%)
  • Malaria: 12 diagnoses (16.9%)
  • Genital infections: 8 diagnoses (11.3%)
  • Diabetes: 7 diagnoses (9.9%)
  • Helminthiasis: 5 diagnoses (7.0%)

Clinical Note: Hypertension and diabetes were identified as priority chronic conditions requiring longitudinal follow-up and formalized referral pathways.

B. Sickle Cell Screening & Counseling

Delivered in partnership with Tackle Sickle Cell Africa (TSCA), 76 community members underwent screening and counseling:

  • Normal haemoglobin (HbAA): 53 individuals (69.7%)
  • Sickle cell trait (HbAS): 20 individuals (26.3%)
  • Sickle cell disease (HbSS): 3 individuals (3.9%)

In total, 23 people (30.3% of those screened) were identified carrying either the sickle cell trait or disease. Knowledge of sickle cell status equips these individuals and families to make informed reproductive health decisions.

C. HIV Screening & Prevention Services

Delivered with The AIDS Support Organisation (TASO), Gulu Centre:

  • Community reach: 56 people reached with education and counseling; 52 tested.
  • Negative results: 51 clients (98.1%) tested negative.
  • Positive results: 1 client (1.9%) tested positive and was immediately linked to psychosocial care and clinical management.
  • PrEP initiation: 3 high-risk individuals were successfully started on Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis.
  • Counseling: 1 client received counseling only.

D. Cervical Cancer Screening

Delivered with Reproductive Health Uganda (RHU), Gulu Branch:

  • Total women screened: 35 women.
  • Age distribution: Under 30 years: 12 (34.3%); 30–35 years: 5 (14.3%); Over 35 years: 18 (51.4%).
  • Referral outcome: 1 woman (2.9%) was identified with suspicious clinical lesions and linked to secondary assessment and management.

E. Modern Family Planning Support

Delivered with RHU Gulu Branch, supporting 13 women (ages 17–45):

  • Age groups: 17–20 yrs: 5 (38.5%); 21–25 yrs: 3 (23.1%); 26–45 yrs: 5 (38.5%).
  • Method choice: Depo-Provera (Injectable): 11 (84.6%); Sayana Press (Subcutaneous): 2 (15.4%).

6. Operational Lessons Learned

  1. Demand outstripped capacity: Community turnout was higher than anticipated, resulting in clinician bottlenecks and waiting times across registration, consultation, and pharmacy points.
  2. Supplies ran short: Increased patient volume depleted certain curative and preventive drugs before the end of the day.
  3. Structured patient flow needed: The absence of an early triage station caused acutely ill patients to mix with preventive screening clients, causing registration congestion.
  4. Single-day model limits depth: A one-day outreach restricted the time available for thorough one-on-one counseling at each service desk.

7. Next-Phase Strategic Priorities

  1. Fund more clinicians at consultation points to cut client waiting times and avoid team exhaustion.
  2. Procure and quantify supplies earlier, ensuring adequate buffer stocks for essential drugs and reagents.
  3. Build structured referral and follow-up pathways for patients diagnosed with sickle cell, hypertension, diabetes, and cervical abnormalities.
  4. Expand cervical cancer screening and family planning outreach to increase uptake in rural communities.
  5. Train staff and volunteers in documentation tools to ensure complete digital and paper audit accuracy.
  6. Replicate the multi-partner outreach model at additional health centres across the Acholi sub-region.
